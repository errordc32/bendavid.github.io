## The problem

Hearing aids are tiny. For someone with arthritis, tremor, or limited hand strength, just picking one up and putting it in can be the hardest part of using it. Many older adults are in exactly this situation, and some stop wearing their hearing aids because handling them is too frustrating. That leads to social isolation, which is linked to faster cognitive decline.

Through TOM at UCSD, I designed a **wearable handling aid** so they can do it without help.

## Design requirements

- **No pinch grip required.** The user should hold and guide the aid with large, whole-hand movements.
- **Stays on the hand** without the user having to hold onto it.
- **Secure but gentle hold** on the hearing aid, so it doesn't drop but releases once it's seated in the ear.
- **Comfortable** for daily wear and easy to put on with one hand.
- **Cheap to make** and easy to resize for different hands.

## Design

{% include media.html src="/assets/images/projects/hear_handle/hearing_aid_tool.png" class="small" caption="CAD of the clip fitted to a hand model, with the hearing-aid post on the palm side." %}

- A **C-shaped clip** wraps around the base of the index finger and rests in the web of the thumb, so it stays put through the hand's own geometry and needs no grip.
- A **post on the palm side** holds the hearing aid in a fixed position. The user brings the hand toward the ear and the post guides the aid into place.
- The clip was **modeled around a hand scan**, so it follows the natural contours of the palm.
- It's designed for **3D printing**, so it can be scaled for different hand sizes and reprinted cheaply.

## Next steps

1. **Print fit-test prototypes** in a few sizes.
2. **Test with users:** comfort, ease of putting it on, and whether it actually makes insertion easier.
3. **Iterate** on the post's grip and release.
4. **Release the files** as open source through TOM.
