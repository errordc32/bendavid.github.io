## The need

Young children with spina bifida often need a wheelchair before they're old enough to fit commercial pediatric models. Those chairs are **expensive**, and kids **outgrow them quickly**. Insurance approval can take months, which is a long time in a toddler's development. Being able to move independently early on matters for physical, cognitive, and social development.

## Design requirements

| Requirement | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Lightweight | Small children need to be able to self-propel |
| Strong and durable | Daily use, bumps, and transport in cars |
| Low cost | Families shouldn't have to wait on insurance |
| Printable on consumer printers | Anyone with a printer can reproduce it from the open-source files |
| Safe seating | Harness, side support, and a stable base to prevent tipping |

## Design

The chair is printed in **PETG**. It's tougher and more impact-resistant than PLA, handles warm cars better, and is still easy to print. The seat shell is a single contoured piece with integrated side supports. The large rear wheels are placed so a small child can reach the rims, and front casters let the chair turn tightly indoors.

## Fabrication & delivery

Partnering with **Rady Children's Hospital** meant every chair was fitted to a specific child. The team printed and assembled the parts, checked each chair for fit and stability, and delivered them to families.

## Outcome

The wheelchairs are currently still in use, we get frequents messages and videos from their users led to **3 more requests** from families in the San Diego community. TOM also built a [plywood version]({{ '/projects/wheelchair-wood/' | relative_url }}) of the same chair.

## What I took away

Designing for a real child is very different from designing for a spec sheet. Feedback from families, like how the chair fits in a car or whether a child can reach the wheels, shaped the design more than any calculation did.
