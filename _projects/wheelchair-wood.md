## Overview

Alongside the [3D-printed wheelchair]({{ '/projects/wheelchair-3d-printed/' | relative_url }}), TOM at UCSD built a **plywood version** of the pediatric chair for 2 patients with spina bifida. Plywood takes less specialized equipment to build, so more makers can produce it.

## Why a second version?

A 3D-printed chair needs printers and long print times. Many makerspaces, schools, and community shops don't have big print farms, but they **do** have a CNC router or a jigsaw and a sheet of plywood. A plywood version opens the design to many more builders and gives families another option.

## Design

{% include media.html src="/assets/images/projects/pediatric_wheelchairs/wheelchair_1.png" class="small" caption="The plywood wheelchair." %}

- **Slot-and-tab construction:** the frame is flat panels that interlock, so it can be cut from sheet stock and assembled with few fasteners.
- **Curved side panels** double as the structure and as side guards, keeping the child's arms clear of the wheels.
- **Padded seat and five-point harness** for secure, supportive seating.
- **Adjustable footrest:** the slots in the side panels let the footrest move as the child grows.
- **Large rear wheels and front casters**, so the child can self-propel and turn tightly indoors.

## Plywood vs. 3D-printed

| | Plywood | 3D-printed (PETG) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main tools | CNC router or hand tools | FDM 3D printer |
| Build time | Hours of cutting and assembly | Long unattended prints, then assembly |
| Weight | Heavier | Lighter |
| Look & feel | Warm, furniture-like | Smooth, colorful, molded |
| Best for | Shops with woodworking tools | Makerspaces with printers |

## Outcome

Two children with spina bifida received plywood chairs. Together with the 3D-printed version, this rounds out TOM's open-source wheelchair program, so a family's access to a chair doesn't depend on what equipment their local makers have.
